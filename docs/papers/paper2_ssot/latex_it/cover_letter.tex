\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Zero-Error Recovery from Deterministic Partial Views,'' for publication in the Journal of Computer and System Sciences.

The manuscript develops a graph-theoretic theory of zero-error recovery from deterministic partial views of finite latent tuples. Admissible coordinate-subset views induce a confusability graph on latent states, so exact recovery becomes a coloring problem. On the full labeled tuple space, the realizable confusability relations are characterized exactly as those determined by upward-closed families of coordinate-agreement sets. Under block composition, the one-shot confusability graph passes to strong powers and the normalized rates converge to Shannon capacity.

The paper also isolates a structural equality route for the induced graph class: transitive confusability yields a collapse to the cluster-graph case, with meet-witnessing and fiber coherence as sufficient conditions. Under an affine restriction on the realized state family, the coordinate side carries a representable matroid whose rank gives tractable upper bounds on confusability and capacity. The submission combines graph theory, information theory, and structural complexity in a single exact recovery problem, which is why I believe it is well suited to JCSS.

All theorem-level claims cited in the manuscript are machine-checked in Lean 4. An archived Zenodo record containing the formalization, manuscript PDF, and submission source bundle is available at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19354075}. The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

\closing{Sincerely,}
