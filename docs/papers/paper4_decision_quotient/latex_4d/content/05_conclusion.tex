\section{Conclusion}

The frontier question now has both a positive and a negative answer. On the positive side, the tractable landscape studied here factors into primitive mechanisms, lifts, and degeneracies. Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct-profile-compression} shows that this positive side remains substantive at the representation level: bounded distinct action profiles compress to bounded-action slices without changing exact certification, so the existing bounded-actions theorem already yields a genuine structural classifier on that subcase. Section~6 proves that the framework is not confined to native decision outputs. It captures the exact structural-sufficiency problem for standard exact TCS semantics, including decision, counting, search, and exact approximation specifications. On the negative side, quotient realizability is maximal, so quotient shape cannot classify tractability by itself, and an admissibility-layer collapse theorem rules out direct finite classifiers even after imposing theorem-forced closure laws together with computational and structural restrictions. Moreover, Theorem~\ref{thm:closure-sound-package-no-go} isolates the load-bearing hypothesis: the collapse depends only on closure-law invariance, so any guardrail package whose admissible predicates respect this single forced invariance inherits the impossibility. Theorem~\ref{thm:correct-classifier-forces-invariance} strengthens the conclusion further: exact correctness itself forces closure-orbit agreement, and Corollary~\ref{cor:no-correct-tractability-classifier} makes the scope explicit. Proposition~\ref{prop:admissibility-inhabited} shows that the admissibility layer is nonempty, while Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct-profile-compression} shows that the positive classification problem itself is nontrivial. The no-go is therefore universal over correct classifiers on closure-closed domains, not merely over classifiers that build invariance into their definitions.

Technically, the mechanism is closure-orbit disagreement via pair-targeted action-independent affine transport. The same template now applies to four obstruction families (dominant pair, margin masking, ghost action, and additive/statewise offset), yielding an admissible-collapse theorem.

The open problem is now sharper. Simple finite lists and unconstrained quotient predicates cannot characterize the boundary, and admissible closure-invariant classifiers of the present kind cannot do so either. Any successful frontier theorem must therefore invoke stronger representation-sensitive structure than the one ruled out here.
