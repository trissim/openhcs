\section{Conclusion}

The frontier question now has both a positive and a negative answer. On the positive side, the tractable landscape studied here factors into primitive mechanisms, lifts, and degeneracies. Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct-profile-compression} shows that this positive side remains substantive at the representation level: bounded distinct action profiles compress to bounded-action slices without changing exact certification, so the existing bounded-actions theorem already yields a genuine structural classifier on that subcase. Section~6 shows that the framework is not confined to native decision outputs. It captures the structural-sufficiency problem for every output specification expressible as a state-indexed admissible-output relation, and every exact specification admits a coordinate presentation, with a finite Boolean one in the finite-state case. This includes decision, counting, search, approximation guarantees, PAC/regret/risk guarantees, finite-horizon or anytime guarantees, randomized-output guarantees, and distributional specifications once written in that form. Proposition~\ref{prop:semantic-claims-depend-only-on-output-equivalence}, Corollary~\ref{cor:every-state-equivalence-relation-is-realizable-as-exact-output-semantics}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:semantically-extensional-claims-factor-through-the-quotient} sharpen that point: semantic claims depend only on admissible-output equivalence, every state equivalence relation is already realizable at the output-semantics layer, and semantically extensional claims factor through that quotient. The Lean development also formalizes the compute-cost closure-orbit layer: optimizer computation, deterministic optimizer-set payload output, admissible-output search, external output objects, and representation-relative transported outputs such as policies and randomized procedures all admit theorem-backed closure transport, and correctness of a polynomial-time classifier again forces closure-orbit agreement. The approximation layer is now sharpened as well: witness-gap control preserves local relevance and non-sufficiency witnesses, a uniform strict-gap hypothesis preserves the whole optimizer quotient, and arbitrarily small perturbations can otherwise flip the exact judgment. Taken together, these results constrain tractability classification at the level of computational specifications rather than at the level of any one output formalism. On the negative side, quotient realizability is maximal, so quotient shape cannot classify tractability by itself, and an admissibility-layer collapse theorem rules out direct finite classifiers even after imposing theorem-forced closure laws together with computational and structural restrictions. Moreover, Theorem~\ref{thm:closure-sound-package-no-go} isolates the load-bearing hypothesis: the collapse depends only on closure-law invariance, so any guardrail package whose admissible predicates respect this single forced invariance inherits the impossibility. Theorem~\ref{thm:correct-classifier-forces-invariance} strengthens the conclusion further: correctness itself forces closure-orbit agreement, and Corollary~\ref{cor:no-correct-tractability-classifier} gives the resulting universal-scope no-go for correct classifiers on closure-closed domains.

Technically, the mechanism is closure-orbit disagreement via pair-targeted action-independent affine transport. The same template now applies to four obstruction families (dominant pair, margin masking, ghost action, and additive/statewise offset), yielding an admissible-collapse theorem. The dominant-pair witness also yields a concrete compute-cost no-go: no correct classifier for polynomial-time optimizer computation can decide dominant-pair status exactly, so the compute-cost layer fires on the obstruction families in the same theorem-backed way as the admissible-predicate layer.

The open problem is now sharper. Simple finite lists and unconstrained quotient predicates cannot characterize the boundary, and admissible closure-invariant classifiers of the present kind cannot do so either. Any successful frontier theorem must therefore invoke stronger representation-sensitive structure than the one ruled out here.
