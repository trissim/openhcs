Every exact computational specification admits a coordinate presentation, and every finite-state exact specification admits a finite Boolean one. Section~6 then proves that decision, counting, search, approximation, PAC/regret/risk, randomized-output, finite-horizon or anytime, and distributional specifications all reduce to the same coordinate-dependence problem. The tractable families treated here admit a finite primitive basis, but optimizer-quotient realizability is maximal, so quotient shape alone cannot characterize the frontier.

We prove a meta-impossibility theorem for efficiently checkable structural predicates invariant under the theorem-forced closure laws of exact certification. Structural convergence with zero-distortion summaries, quotient entropy bounds, and support-counting arguments explain why those laws are canonical. The theorem is established by same-orbit disagreements for four obstruction families: dominant-pair concentration, margin masking, ghost-action concentration, and additive/statewise offset concentration, using action-independent pair-targeted affine witnesses. Consequently no correct tractability classifier on a closure-closed domain yields an exact characterization over these families. More generally, restricting to a closure-closed subdomain helps only by removing orbit gaps. Uniform strict-gap control preserves the full optimizer quotient, while arbitrarily small perturbations can otherwise flip relevance and sufficiency. Closure-orbit agreement is forced by correctness, and the same compute-cost barrier extends from optimizer computation to canonical payload/search tasks and theorem-backed external or transported outputs. The obstruction therefore appears at the level of computational specifications themselves, not any particular output formalism.
