Exact relevance certification asks which coordinates are necessary to determine the optimal action in a coordinate-structured decision problem. We first give a positive structural consolidation: the tractable families considered here split into primitive mechanisms, regime lifts, and degenerate collapses, yielding a finite primitive basis. We then prove a realizability barrier: arbitrary labeling kernels are optimizer-realizable, so quotient shape alone cannot characterize a tractability frontier.

A meta-impossibility theorem is proved for an admissibility class of normalization predicates. These predicates are required to be polynomial-time checkable, structural, and invariant under theorem-forced closure laws. Invariance follows from structural convergence: zero-distortion summaries, quotient entropy bounds, and Fisher-style support counts already factor through the same optimizer-quotient core. Within this class, same-orbit disagreement witnesses are constructed for four obstruction families, namely dominant-pair concentration, margin masking, ghost-action concentration, and additive/statewise offset concentration, using action-independent, pair-targeted affine terms. It follows that no admissible normalization predicate yields an exact tractability characterization over these families. The theorem extends Rice/Arrow-style impossibility reasoning to representation-sensitive classifiers.
