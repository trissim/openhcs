\section{Introduction}

Exact relevance certification asks: given a coordinate-structured decision problem, which coordinates are necessary to determine the optimal action set? This question appears whenever one seeks principled feature deletion or abstraction while preserving optimization behavior. Under succinct encodings, exact certification is hard in the ambient regimes (coNP-complete in static settings and PSPACE-complete in sequential settings), so any tractability statement must be genuinely structural.

This paper should be read as the meta-level companion to the optimizer-quotient and certification-trilemma line of work~\cite{simas2025optimizerquotient}. That line isolates the semantic object that exact certification depends on and maps the surrounding complexity landscape across regimes. The present paper asks a different question: once that semantic core is fixed, can one still hope for a finite structural tractability classifier at the representation level? The answer established here is negative for the admissibility class developed below.

The current landscape is already rich enough to separate two distinct tasks. The first is ``positive consolidation'': identify and organize known polynomial-time islands. The second is ``meta-frontier control'': determine whether a finite structural classifier can exist at all once one respects the invariances forced by exact-certification semantics. This paper addresses both tasks.

On the positive side, we prove that the tractable families treated here split into three roles: primitive core mechanisms, regime lifts, and degenerate collapses. This yields a finite primitive basis. On its own, however, this does not establish a frontier theorem: finite inventory is not finite characterization.

The natural next move is to classify by optimizer-induced quotient structure. We show this is impossible in the unconstrained sense: optimizer realizability is maximal, and arbitrary labeling kernels are realizable. Thus quotient shape is too expressive to carry the boundary by itself.

This realizability barrier motivates the organizing principle of the paper: tractability predicates must be evaluated on representation-level structural classes, but modulo theorem-forced equivalences of presentation. In particular, closure under relabelings, positive affine utility reparameterizations, duplication, and irrelevant-coordinate extension is not a modeling preference; it is inherited from the fact that exact certification depends only on the induced decision quotient relation.

At the same time, unrestricted predicates trivialize any impossibility theorem via oracle encoding. We therefore isolate a principled admissibility layer: polynomial-time checkability, structural extractability, closure-law invariance, and bounded-pattern definability. This class excludes direct semantic encoding while still covering the structural results established here.

Within this admissibility class, we prove a four-family no-go template. For each obstruction family we construct two slices with different obstruction status in the same closure orbit. The key mechanism is an action-independent, pair-targeted additive affine term (the statewise ``$\alpha$-component'' of positive affine transport), which changes the obstruction predicate while preserving closure equivalence. Closure-law invariance then transports any candidate predicate across the orbit and forces contradiction.

The dominant-pair family is the canonical instance, and the same mechanism now applies to margin masking, ghost-action concentration, and additive/statewise offset concentration. The resulting theorem states that no admissible normalization predicate can serve as a tractability characterization in the required sense.

Conceptually, the result belongs to the same meta-impossibility lineage as Rice-style and Arrow-style theorems: once one accepts a natural invariance package and a nontrivial expressivity domain, the desired classifier cannot exist in the naive finite form. What remains open is not whether arbitrary finite classifiers fail, but what stronger structural principle (if any) yields a correct frontier theorem.
