\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am pleased to submit the manuscript ``Toward a Tractability Frontier for Exact Relevance Certification'' for consideration in TheoretiCS.

The paper studies whether exact relevance certification admits a finite optimizer-compatible tractability classifier at the representation level. It first gives a positive structural consolidation: the tractable families treated in the paper split into primitive mechanisms, regime lifts, and degenerate collapses, yielding a finite primitive basis. It then proves a realizability barrier showing that arbitrary labeling kernels are optimizer-realizable, so quotient shape alone cannot characterize the frontier.

The main theorem is a meta-impossibility result for an admissibility class of normalization predicates. Closure-law invariance is justified both by theorem-forced semantic invariance and by convergence with zero-distortion, entropy, and support-counting viewpoints. Within that class, the paper constructs same-orbit disagreement witnesses for four obstruction families---dominant-pair concentration, margin masking, ghost-action concentration, and additive/statewise offset concentration---using a common pair-targeted affine mechanism. It follows that no admissible normalization predicate yields an exact tractability characterization. This is a theorem-centered contribution in complexity theory and formalized mathematics, and it is intended to fit TheoretiCS as a self-contained mathematical paper rather than as an artifact-driven submission.

All theorem-level claims cited in the manuscript are mechanically verified in Lean 4. The archived artifact, manuscript PDF, and submission source bundle are available at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19457896}. The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

\closing{Sincerely,}
