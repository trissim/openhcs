\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Toward a Tractability Frontier for Exact Relevance Certification,'' for publication in TheoretiCS.

The manuscript studies whether exact relevance certification admits a finite optimizer-compatible tractability classifier at the representation level. It first gives a finite structural consolidation of the known tractable families. It then proves a realizability barrier showing that arbitrary labeling kernels are optimizer-realizable, so quotient shape alone cannot characterize the frontier.

The main theorem is a meta-impossibility result for an admissibility class of normalization predicates. Closure-law invariance is justified by theorem-forced semantic invariance and by convergence with zero-distortion, entropy, and support-counting viewpoints. Within that class, the manuscript constructs same-orbit disagreement witnesses for four obstruction families, namely dominant-pair concentration, margin masking, ghost-action concentration, and additive/statewise offset concentration, using a common pair-targeted affine mechanism. It follows that no admissible normalization predicate yields an exact tractability characterization over these families. The submission is intended as a theorem-centered complexity paper with a self-contained mathematical presentation.

All theorem-level claims cited in the manuscript are mechanically verified in Lean 4. The archived artifact, manuscript PDF, and submission source bundle are available at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19457896}. The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

\closing{Sincerely,}
