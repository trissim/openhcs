Which coordinates of a decision problem can you hide without changing the regime-induced optimizer? The answer depends on how the question is posed. We show that exact relevance certification has fundamentally different complexity in three natural settings: static (ruling out counterexamples), stochastic (sufficiency under coarser conditioning together with a companion decisiveness family), and sequential (certifying an induced optimizer in a temporally structured model). For a decision problem $\mathcal{D}=(A,S,U)$, this yields the decision problems \textsc{Sufficiency-Check}, \textsc{Minimum-Sufficient-Set}, and \textsc{Anchor-Sufficiency}, together with the optimizer quotient as the coarsest abstraction that preserves optimal-action distinctions.

The central result is a theorem-backed benchmark matrix across regimes. In the static regime, minimum sufficiency collapses to relevance containment, placing exact minimization in coNP rather than the expected $\Sigma_2^P$. In the stochastic regime, sufficiency is polynomial-time under explicit-state encoding and comes with mechanized bridges back to static sufficiency and the optimizer quotient; the companion stochastic decisiveness family is polynomial-time under explicit-state encoding and, under succinct encoding, is PP-hard with a mechanized scoped complement upper bound of coNP-over-PP style; and stochastic anchor and minimum decisiveness queries carry PP-hardness together with scoped witness-over-PP upper bounds supporting their $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$ placement. In the sequential regime, sufficiency, minimum, and anchor queries are PSPACE-complete.

We prove an encoding-sensitive contrast between explicit-state tractability and succinct-encoding hardness, and we identify an integrity-competence trilemma: no exact certifier can simultaneously be sound, complete, and polynomial-budgeted on the hard regime. We also isolate six structural conditions under which exact certification remains polynomial. This establishes both tractability islands and fundamental limits for exact simplification.

The Lean 4 artifact certifies the core reduction-correctness lemmas, size-bounded hardness packages, exact finite deciders, explicit-state step-counting upper-bound wrappers, the stochastic sufficiency bridge package, the scoped decisiveness upper bounds, and the internal completeness-style layers supporting the stochastic existential classification. This yields a mechanically checked core for the reduction and finite-decision layers of the paper.
