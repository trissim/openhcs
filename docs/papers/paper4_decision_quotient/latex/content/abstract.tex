Which coordinates of a decision problem can you hide without changing the decision? The answer depends on how the question is posed. We show that exact relevance certification has fundamentally different complexity in three natural settings: static (ruling out counterexamples), stochastic (certification under conditioning and expectation), and sequential (certifying an induced optimizer in a temporally structured model). For a decision problem $\mathcal{D}=(A,S,U)$, this yields the decision problems \textsc{Sufficiency-Check}, \textsc{Minimum-Sufficient-Set}, and \textsc{Anchor-Sufficiency}, together with the optimizer quotient as the coarsest abstraction that preserves optimal-action distinctions.

The central result is a regime-sensitive complexity matrix across the three settings. In the static regime, minimum sufficiency collapses to relevance containment, placing exact minimization in coNP rather than the expected $\Sigma_2^P$. In the stochastic regime, the exact question separates into stochastic sufficiency, which asks whether coarse conditioning preserves the full-information optimizer, and stochastic decisiveness, which asks whether each observed fiber admits a unique conditional optimum. For stochastic preservation, we prove explicit-state tractability and bridge theorems to the static theory; under full support, the minimum and anchor variants inherit the static classifications, while the general-distribution classification remains open. For stochastic decisiveness, the base query is polynomial-time under explicit-state encoding and, under succinct encoding, is PP-hard with a mechanized oracle-style complement upper bound; its anchor and minimum variants are PP-hard and lie in $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$. In the sequential regime, sufficiency, minimum, and anchor queries are PSPACE-complete.

We prove an encoding-sensitive contrast between explicit-state tractability and succinct-encoding hardness, and we identify an integrity-competence trilemma: no exact certifier can simultaneously be sound, complete, and polynomial-budgeted on the hard regime. We also isolate six structural conditions under which exact certification remains polynomial. This establishes both tractability islands and fundamental limits for exact simplification.
