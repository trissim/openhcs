\section{Regime Matrix}\label{sec:regime-hierarchy}

The static, stochastic, and sequential formulations are the paper's central organizing object. They ask the same underlying question about which coordinates matter for the decision, but they answer it in increasingly expressive models. Probability does not merely raise the static question; it bifurcates it into two inequivalent exact predicates: preservation and determinacy. Adding time then introduces temporal contingency. Read as a complexity matrix, adding probability introduces conditional-comparison hardness, and adding time lifts the core queries further to PSPACE.

\subsection{Complexity Matrix}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Regime / predicate & Base query & Minimum & Anchor \\
\midrule
Static sufficiency & coNP-complete & coNP-complete & $\Sigma_2^P$-complete \\
Stochastic preservation & P (explicit), bridge theorems & coNP-complete (full support) / open (general) & $\Sigma_2^P$-complete (full support) / open (general) \\
Stochastic decisiveness & P (explicit), PP-hard + scoped upper bound (succinct) & PP-hard, in $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$ & PP-hard, in $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$ \\
Sequential sufficiency & PSPACE-complete & PSPACE-complete & PSPACE-complete \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This table is the paper's compact summary. In the static regime, sufficiency and minimum sufficiency are coNP-complete and anchor sufficiency is $\Sigma_2^P$-complete. In the stochastic regime, probability bifurcates the exact question into preservation and determinacy. For preservation (stochastic sufficiency), the paper establishes explicit-state tractability and bridge theorems back to static sufficiency and the optimizer quotient; under full support, the minimum and anchor variants inherit coNP-completeness and $\Sigma_2^P$-completeness from the static regime, while the general-distribution classification of those variants remains open. For determinacy (stochastic decisiveness), the paper establishes the full classification: polynomial-time in the explicit-state model, PP-hardness plus a mechanized oracle-style complement upper bound in the succinct model, and $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$ membership for the anchor and minimum variants. In the sequential regime, sufficiency, minimum, and anchor queries are PSPACE-complete.

\paragraph{Preservation Boundary.}
For stochastic sufficiency in the preservation sense, this paper proves explicit-state tractability (Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-preservation-explicit}), static-to-stochastic bridge theorems (Propositions~\ref{prop:stochastic-to-static-sufficiency} and~\ref{prop:stochastic-full-support-equiv}), quotient equivalence under full support (Proposition~\ref{prop:stochastic-quotient-equiv}), and full-support inheritance for the minimum and anchor variants (Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-preservation-full-support-variants}). Outside the full-support setting, this paper does \emph{not} claim a succinct-encoding hardness classification for the preservation predicate or a general-distribution classification for its minimum and anchor variants. The PP-hardness results in the stochastic row concern decisiveness and its variants, not preservation itself.

The decisiveness results combine standard hardness claims with mechanized oracle-style upper-bound packages. Those packages support, but do not replace, the conventional oracle-class interpretations stated in the text.

The stochastic anchor and minimum decisiveness queries are not presently collapsed to PP-completeness: Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-nppp-upper} places them in $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$, while Theorems~\ref{thm:stochastic-anchor-hard} and~\ref{thm:stochastic-minimum-hard} give PP-hardness. The artifact mechanizes the witness-over-verifier structure and explicit-state bounded-witness recoveries behind that upper-bound story, and it also packages existential-majority hardness/completeness surrogates for the anchor and decisiveness query families \leanmeta{\LHrng{EH}{2}{10}.} It still does not package a full oracle-class membership proof under the succinct encoding. By contrast, the sequential anchor and minimum variants admit paper-level PSPACE membership via guess-and-verify over anchors or coordinate subsets together with the sequential sufficiency verifier.

\begin{theorem}[Regime-Sensitive Complexity Matrix]\label{thm:regime-main}
Under the encoding conventions of Section~\ref{sec:encoding}, the classification is as follows. In the static regime, sufficiency and minimum sufficiency are coNP-complete and anchor sufficiency is $\Sigma_2^P$-complete. In the stochastic regime, probability bifurcates the exact question into preservation and determinacy. For preservation (stochastic sufficiency), the base query is polynomial-time under explicit-state encoding with proved bridges back to static sufficiency and the optimizer quotient; under full support, the minimum and anchor variants are coNP-complete and $\Sigma_2^P$-complete, respectively. For determinacy (stochastic decisiveness), the base query is polynomial-time under explicit-state encoding and, under succinct encoding, is PP-hard with a mechanized oracle-style complement upper bound; the anchor and minimum decisiveness queries are PP-hard and lie in $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$. In the sequential regime, sufficiency, minimum, and anchor queries are PSPACE-complete.
\end{theorem}

\leanmeta{\LH{DC100}, \LH{DQ1}, \LHrng{DQ}{17}{19}, \LHrng{OU}{1}{2}, \LHrng{OU}{11}{12}, \LHrng{EH}{4}{10}.}

\begin{proof}
The matrix entries are exactly Theorems~\ref{thm:sufficiency-conp}, \ref{thm:minsuff-conp}, \ref{thm:anchor-sigma2p}, \ref{thm:stochastic-preservation-explicit}, \ref{thm:stochastic-preservation-full-support-variants}, \ref{thm:stochastic-pp}, \ref{thm:stochastic-anchor-hard}, \ref{thm:stochastic-minimum-hard}, \ref{thm:stochastic-nppp-upper}, \ref{thm:sequential-pspace}, \ref{thm:sequential-anchor-hard}, and \ref{thm:sequential-minimum-hard}, together with the stochastic sufficiency bridge package. The strict regime comparisons are isolated separately in Theorems~\ref{thm:static-stochastic-strict} and~\ref{thm:stochastic-sequential-strict}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strict Regime Gaps}

\begin{theorem}[Static-to-Stochastic Complexity Gap]\label{thm:static-stochastic-strict}
Assuming $\coNP \ne \PP$, the succinct stochastic decisiveness classification is strictly harder in worst-case complexity than the static sufficiency classification.
\end{theorem}

\leanmeta{\LH{DQ18}.}

\begin{proof}
Static sufficiency checking is coNP-complete (Theorem~\ref{thm:sufficiency-conp}), while stochastic decisiveness is PP-hard under the succinct encoding (Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-pp}). Under the assumption $\coNP \ne \PP$, this yields a strict complexity gap between the two classifications.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stochastic-to-Sequential Complexity Gap]\label{thm:stochastic-sequential-strict}
Assuming $\PP \ne \PSPACE$, the sequential sufficiency classification is strictly harder in worst-case complexity than the stochastic decisiveness classification.
\end{theorem}

\leanmeta{\LH{DQ19}.}

\begin{proof}
Stochastic decisiveness is PP-hard under the succinct encoding (Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-pp}), while sequential sufficiency is PSPACE-complete (Theorem~\ref{thm:sequential-pspace}). The class gap yields a strict complexity separation between the two classifications under the assumption $\PP \ne \PSPACE$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Integrity-Resource Bounds}

The regime matrix has a methodological consequence as well: as one moves upward in the hierarchy, exact certification becomes less compatible with fixed polynomial resource budgets.

\begin{proposition}[Abstention Frontier Expands with Regime Complexity]\label{prop:abstention-frontier}
For polynomial-time exact certifiers that abstain whenever they cannot certify correctness within budget, the set of instances requiring abstention expands along
\[
\text{static} \to \text{stochastic} \to \text{sequential}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\leanmeta{\LH{IC32}, \LH{DQ2}.}

\begin{proof}
As the decision predicate moves from coNP to PP to PSPACE hardness, exact worst-case certification requires strictly stronger resources. Fixed polynomial resources therefore force abstention on a larger instance family in higher regimes.
\end{proof}
