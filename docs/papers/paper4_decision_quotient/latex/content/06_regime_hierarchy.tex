\section{Regime Matrix}\label{sec:regime-hierarchy}

The static, stochastic, and sequential formulations are the paper's central organizing object. They ask the same underlying question about which coordinates matter for the decision, but they answer it in increasingly expressive models. The stochastic row naturally splits in two: stochastic sufficiency, which is the closest analogue of static sufficiency, and stochastic decisiveness, which underlies the strongest stochastic complexity theorems and the current mechanization. Read as a complexity matrix, adding probability introduces conditional-comparison hardness, and adding time lifts the benchmark queries further to PSPACE.

\subsection{Complexity Matrix}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Regime & Sufficiency & Minimum & Anchor \\
\midrule
Static & coNP-complete & coNP-complete & $\Sigma_2^P$-complete \\
Stochastic sufficiency & P (explicit), static/quotient bridge under full support & bridge only & bridge only \\
Stochastic decisiveness & P (explicit), PP-hard + scoped coNP-over-PP upper bound (succinct) & PP-hard + scoped NP-over-PP upper bound & PP-hard + scoped NP-over-PP upper bound \\
Sequential & PSPACE-complete & PSPACE-complete & PSPACE-complete \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This table is the paper's compact summary. Adding probability does not yield one single stochastic theorem package. Instead, it yields stochastic sufficiency, which already has a certified explicit-state tractability theorem and mechanized bridges back to static sufficiency and the optimizer quotient, and a companion decisiveness family for which the paper has the strongest classification and mechanization: polynomial-time in the explicit-state model, PP-hardness plus a mechanized oracle-style complement upper bound in the succinct model, and existential witness-based upper bounds for anchor and minimum. Adding time then lifts the benchmark sequential queries to PSPACE.

The stochastic decisiveness row combines standard hardness claims with mechanized oracle-style upper-bound packages. Those packages support, but do not replace, the conventional oracle-class interpretations stated in the text.

The stochastic anchor and minimum variants are not presently collapsed to PP-completeness: Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-nppp-upper} places them in a witness-based upper-bound framework supporting their conventional $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$ reading, while Theorems~\ref{thm:stochastic-anchor-hard} and~\ref{thm:stochastic-minimum-hard} give PP-hardness. These theorems concern stochastic decisiveness, not stochastic sufficiency itself. The artifact now mechanizes the witness-over-verifier structure and explicit-state bounded-witness recoveries behind that upper-bound story, and it also packages existential-majority hardness/completeness surrogates for the anchor and decisiveness query families \leanmeta{\LHrng{EH}{2}{10}.} It still does not package a full oracle-class membership proof under the succinct encoding. By contrast, the sequential anchor and minimum variants admit paper-level PSPACE membership via guess-and-verify over anchors or coordinate subsets together with the sequential sufficiency verifier.

\begin{theorem}[Benchmark Escalation Matrix]\label{thm:regime-main}
Under the encoding conventions of Section~\ref{sec:encoding}, the benchmark package is as follows. In the static regime, sufficiency and minimum sufficiency are coNP-complete and anchor sufficiency is $\Sigma_2^P$-complete. In the stochastic regime, sufficiency is polynomial-time under the explicit-state encoding and comes with proved bridges back to static sufficiency and the optimizer quotient, while decisiveness is polynomial-time under the explicit-state encoding and, under the succinct encoding, is PP-hard with a mechanized oracle-style complement upper bound; the minimum and anchor decisiveness queries are PP-hard and admit witness-based upper bounds. In the sequential regime, sufficiency, minimum, and anchor queries are PSPACE-complete.
\end{theorem}

\leanmeta{\LH{DC100}, \LH{DQ1}, \LHrng{DQ}{17}{19}, \LHrng{OU}{1}{2}, \LHrng{OU}{11}{12}, \LHrng{EH}{4}{10}.}

\begin{proof}
The matrix entries are exactly Theorems~\ref{thm:sufficiency-conp}, \ref{thm:minsuff-conp}, \ref{thm:anchor-sigma2p}, \ref{thm:stochastic-preservation-explicit}, \ref{thm:stochastic-pp}, \ref{thm:stochastic-anchor-hard}, \ref{thm:stochastic-minimum-hard}, \ref{thm:stochastic-nppp-upper}, \ref{thm:sequential-pspace}, \ref{thm:sequential-anchor-hard}, and \ref{thm:sequential-minimum-hard}, together with the stochastic sufficiency bridge package and the benchmark summary theorem in the artifact. The strict benchmark comparisons are isolated separately in Theorems~\ref{thm:static-stochastic-strict} and~\ref{thm:stochastic-sequential-strict}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strict Benchmark Gaps}

\begin{theorem}[Static-to-Stochastic Complexity Gap]\label{thm:static-stochastic-strict}
Assuming $\coNP \ne \PP$, the succinct stochastic decisiveness benchmark is strictly harder in worst-case complexity than the static sufficiency benchmark.
\end{theorem}

\leanmeta{\LH{DQ18}.}

\begin{proof}
Static sufficiency checking is coNP-complete (Theorem~\ref{thm:sufficiency-conp}), while stochastic decisiveness is PP-hard under the succinct encoding (Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-pp}). Under the assumption $\coNP \ne \PP$, this yields a strict complexity gap between the benchmark problems.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stochastic-to-Sequential Complexity Gap]\label{thm:stochastic-sequential-strict}
Assuming $\PP \ne \PSPACE$, the sequential sufficiency benchmark is strictly harder in worst-case complexity than the stochastic decisiveness benchmark.
\end{theorem}

\leanmeta{\LH{DQ19}.}

\begin{proof}
Stochastic decisiveness is PP-hard under the succinct encoding (Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-pp}), while sequential sufficiency is PSPACE-complete (Theorem~\ref{thm:sequential-pspace}). The class gap yields a strict complexity separation between the benchmark problems under the assumption $\PP \ne \PSPACE$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Integrity-Resource Bounds}

The benchmark matrix has a methodological consequence as well: as one moves upward in the benchmark hierarchy, exact certification becomes less compatible with fixed polynomial resource budgets.

\begin{proposition}[Abstention Frontier Expands with Regime Complexity]\label{prop:abstention-frontier}
For polynomial-time exact certifiers that abstain whenever they cannot certify correctness within budget, the set of instances requiring abstention expands along
\[
\text{static} \to \text{stochastic} \to \text{sequential}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\leanmeta{\LH{IC32}, \LH{DQ2}.}

\begin{proof}
As the decision predicate moves from coNP to PP to PSPACE hardness, exact worst-case certification requires strictly stronger resources. Fixed polynomial resources therefore force abstention on a larger instance family in higher regimes.
\end{proof}
