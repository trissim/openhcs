\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Exact decision-preserving information has a canonical form: the optimizer quotient. We study finite coordinate-structured decision problems $\mathcal{D}=(A,S,U)$ with $S = X_1 \times \cdots \times X_n$, and ask which coordinates can be hidden without changing the optimal-action correspondence. A coordinate set $I$ is sufficient when agreement on $I$ forces agreement on the optimal-action set:
\[
s_I = s'_I \implies \Opt(s) = \Opt(s').
\]

The optimizer quotient is the coarsest exact decision-preserving abstraction: every surjective abstraction that preserves optimal actions factors uniquely through it. Equivalently, in \textbf{Set}, it is the coimage/image factorization of the optimizer map. This canonical status is theorem-level in the artifact (Theorems~2.4--2.5), and it is the organizing object of the paper.

From this object we derive a regime-sensitive complexity landscape. In the static regime, certification reduces to counterexample exclusion and minimum sufficiency collapses to relevance containment (coNP-complete). In the stochastic regime, conditioning splits the exact question into preservation and decisiveness; preservation bridges to static sufficiency, while decisiveness is PP-hard under succinct encoding. In the sequential regime, temporal contingency lifts exact certification to PSPACE-complete. Six structural restrictions yield polynomial-time certification.

The structural target is the same throughout, but the cost of certifying it changes with the ambient model. \leanmeta{\LH{QT1}, \LH{QT7}.}

This is a certification problem rather than a forward-evaluation problem: computing $\Opt(s)$ at one state is local, while proving irrelevance of coordinates is universal. That quantifier shift drives the hardness results and explains why the quotient perspective is central instead of cosmetic. Throughout, we work under the encoding conventions fixed in Section~\ref{sec:encoding}.

\subsection{Contributions}

The paper is best read as a theory of exact decision-relevant information organized around one canonical mathematical object: the optimizer quotient. The optimizer quotient is the coarsest abstraction that exactly preserves optimal-action distinctions (Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient-universal}), so every exact decision-preserving abstraction must refine it. Building on this canonical object, the paper develops a near-complete, regime-sensitive complexity map for exact relevance certification across the static, stochastic, and sequential models studied here, including twelve explicit cases (six nontrivial tractable subcases), impossibility results, and extensive machine-checked support. The conjecture and open problem are deliberate boundary markers that specify exactly what is settled and what remains open.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Optimizer quotient as canonical structure.} For a finite decision problem, the optimizer quotient identifies states exactly when they induce the same optimal-action set. In \textbf{Set}, it is the coarsest exact decision-preserving abstraction, equivalently the coimage/image factorization of the optimizer map. This gives the paper a common structural object through which the later static, stochastic, and sequential results can be compared.

\item \textbf{Regime-sensitive certification landscape.} The paper classifies exact certification across static, stochastic, and sequential regimes. Static sufficiency yields coNP-complete base and minimum queries (via relevance containment), while static anchor sufficiency is $\Sigma_2^P$-complete. Stochastic analysis splits into preservation and decisiveness: preservation is explicit-state polynomial with full-support inheritance to static classes, while decisiveness is explicit-state polynomial and PP-hard under succinct encoding, with anchor/minimum decisiveness PP-hard and in $\textsf{NP}^{\textsf{PP}}$ at paper level. Sequential sufficiency, minimum, and anchor queries are PSPACE-complete.

\item \textbf{Tractability results.} The paper proves an explicit-state versus succinct-encoding contrast and identifies six natural structural restrictions under which exact certification becomes polynomial-time: bounded actions, separable utility, low tensor rank, tree structure, bounded treewidth, and coordinate symmetry. Each removes a distinct source of hardness.

\item \textbf{Consequences for exact certifiers.} In the hard exact regime, certifiers cannot in general be simultaneously sound, complete, and polynomial-budgeted. The same theory yields witness-checking lower bounds, approximation obstructions, and the practical consequence called the simplicity tax: when exact relevance is not certified, the unresolved burden is not removed but shifted elsewhere in the system.

\item \textbf{Mechanized finite-decision core.} A Lean 4 artifact mechanically checks the optimizer-quotient universal-property core, the main finite deciders, search procedures, reduction-correctness lemmas, witness/checking schemas, and bridge results \leanmeta{\LH{QT1}, \LH{QT7}, \LHrng{DC}{91}{99}, \LHrng{OU}{1}{2}, \LHrng{OU}{8}{12}, \LHrng{EH}{1}{10}.} The oracle-class placements are proved in the paper text, while the artifact independently verifies the finite combinatorial core on which those arguments rely. This places the mechanization in the emerging category of problem-specific certified reduction infrastructure.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quantifier Structure}

Insufficiency has short witnesses (a disagreeing state pair), while sufficiency is universal counterexample exclusion. This quantifier shape explains the static coNP base classification, the static $\Sigma_2^P$ anchor variant, and why stochastic/temporal enrichments raise complexity when conditional comparison or temporal contingency is added.
