Title: Leverage, Structural Rank, and Thermodynamic Selection in Information-Processing Systems

Abstract (Unicode, for Zenodo):
We study leverage as a structural invariant of information-processing systems. For any system A with capabilities Cap(A) > 0, we prove a Five-Way Equivalence:
DOF(A) = 1 ⟺ max leverage L = |Cap|/DOF ⟺ single source of truth ⟺ srank = 1 ⟺ tractable sufficiency ⟺ minimum thermodynamic cost.

We also prove that the England replication inequality,
ΔSₘᵢₙ(k) − ΔSₘᵢₙ(1) ≥ k_Bln k,
reduces to counting: a k-variable system has 2ᵏ states, and the finite inequality k ≤ 2^(k − 1) yields the entropy gap under Landauer calibration. The thermodynamic bound E_decision ≥ srank ⋅ k_BTln 2 then follows from the same calibration (BA7).

Within the explicit thermodynamic model used in the paper (Landauer calibration, finite energy, finite signal speed, and nontrivial state space), we derive a physical no-go theorem for polynomial collapse (LP38). Engineering corollaries follow directly: DOF = n is isomorphic to a series reliability system with n failure points; expected modification cost is proportional to DOF; and minimum edit-energy equals k_BTln 2 ⋅ DOF(A).

Abstract (MathJax, for arXiv):
We study leverage as a structural invariant of information-processing systems. For any system $A$ with capabilities $\mathrm{Cap}(A) > 0$, we prove a Five-Way Equivalence: $$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{DOF}(A) = 1
&\;\iff\; \text{max leverage } L = |\mathrm{Cap}|/\mathrm{DOF}
\;\iff\; \text{single source of truth} \\
&\;\iff\; \mathrm{srank} = 1
\;\iff\; \text{tractable sufficiency} \\
&\;\iff\; \text{minimum thermodynamic cost}.
\end{aligned}$$

We also prove that the England replication inequality, $$\Delta S_{\min}(k) - \Delta S_{\min}(1) \geq k_B \ln k,$$ reduces to counting: a $k$-variable system has $2^k$ states, and the finite inequality $k \leq 2^{k-1}$ yields the entropy gap under Landauer calibration. The thermodynamic bound $E_{\mathrm{decision}} \geq \mathrm{srank} \cdot k_B T \ln 2$ then follows from the same calibration (BA7).

Within the explicit thermodynamic model used in the paper (Landauer calibration, finite energy, finite signal speed, and nontrivial state space), we derive a physical no-go theorem for polynomial collapse (LP38). Engineering corollaries follow directly: $\mathrm{DOF} = n$ is isomorphic to a series reliability system with $n$ failure points; expected modification cost is proportional to $\mathrm{DOF}$; and minimum edit-energy equals $k_B T \ln 2 \cdot \mathrm{DOF}(A)$.
