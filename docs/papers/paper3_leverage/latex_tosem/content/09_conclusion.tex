\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

This paper develops leverage as a software-architecture metric grounded in structural change points rather than stylistic preference. The central software result is that, in fixed capability classes, lower DOF yields lower error probability and lower modification burden, so higher leverage becomes a principled selection criterion rather than an aesthetic slogan.

The framework explains a wide range of familiar design advice in a single structural language. Single source of truth, nominal contracts, generic abstractions, convention over configuration, and normalization all realize the same mechanism: consolidate independently drifting definition loci while preserving capabilities. The OpenHCS example illustrates how that mechanism appears in practice through explicit DOF collapse.

The present TOSEM-oriented split isolates the software-engineering contribution from the mathematical-physics material developed in the companion version of paper~3. The next step for the software paper is empirical strengthening: broader case studies, a reproducible DOF measurement protocol, and a fuller evaluation of threats to validity.
