This paper studies exact class recovery from a noninjective representation $U=\pi(C)$ when the encoder may transmit additional side information. The central primitive is residual ambiguity: once the representation is fixed, exact recovery depends only on how many classes remain unresolved inside its fibers. The contribution is intentionally both exact and unifying. The paper contributes exact coding laws for that residual ambiguity and shows that the same ambiguity-fiber object supports a broader representation-sufficiency theory.

On the theoremic side, the main fixed-length law is exact: the minimum worst-case auxiliary description is determined by the largest representation collision fiber, giving the zero-error rate $\log_2 A_\pi$. The finite-block theorem shows that this budget scales exactly linearly across repeated uses of the same representation.

The paper also gives an average-case counterpart in a representation-adaptive model. When auxiliary description length may depend on the observed representation value, the pointwise optimal fiber budget is the ceiling logarithm of the fiber size, and the expected auxiliary rate is controlled fiber by fiber. The resulting lower and upper bounds place the adaptive problem around $H(C\mid U)$ up to the standard one-bit prefix-coding overhead. Thus the worst-case and adaptive theorems quantify the same residual ambiguity at different resolutions.

The broader structural payoff is representation sufficiency. The same ambiguity-fiber geometry governs when a representation is sufficient for exact downstream tasks, when helper views or retrieval-style augmentation genuinely help, and when modular or factorized latents add exact information rather than architectural redundancy. In the learned-representation reading, the interpretation is direct: injective representations support exact downstream identification with no extra metadata, while noninjective representations impose an irreducible side-information cost.

Query costs and robustness do not begin new stories; they record other ways the same unresolved ambiguity reappears when it is not paid for directly in side-information bits. One can transmit side information to resolve it, or instead build a sufficiently rich helper view out of primitive observables and pay for that view through interactive/query resources; in the structured-axis setting, the matroid law describes the geometry of such minimal sufficiency-restoring augmentations. One can also ask whether an apparently collision-free representation remains safe under world growth. These modalities are different ways of accounting for the same ambiguity left by a noninjective representation.
