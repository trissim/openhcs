\begin{abstract}
We study a zero-error semantic compression problem under a fixed observable family with three resources: tag rate $L$ (bits per entity), identification cost $W$ (attribute queries), and distortion $D$ (misidentification probability). Let $\pi$ be the attribute-profile map and let $A_\pi := \max_u |\{c : \pi(c)=u\}|$ be collision multiplicity. The main compression law is an exact ambiguity converse: any $D=0$ scheme must satisfy $L \ge \log_2 A_\pi$, and this lower bound is tight. At blocklength $t$, the minimum tag budget is $L_t^\star = t\log_2 A_\pi$. The complementary query resource is governed by the distinguishing dimension $d$: without tags ($L=0$), zero-error identification requires $W=\Omega(d)$, and minimal sufficient query sets form the bases of a matroid, making $d$ an invariant of the observation structure. In maximal-barrier domains ($A_\pi=k$), the nominal point $(L,W,D)=(\lceil\log_2 k\rceil,O(1),0)$ is the unique Pareto-optimal zero-error solution. We also study robustness in open worlds and derive operational consequences across databases, model registries, biology, and software runtimes. Supplementary Lean verification accompanies the main claims.

\textbf{Keywords:} semantic compression, zero-error compression, confusability graphs, rate-query tradeoff, matroid structure, zero-error identification
\end{abstract}
