\begin{abstract}
We study a zero-error semantic compression problem under a fixed observable family with three resources: tag rate $L$ (bits per entity), identification cost $W$ (attribute queries), and distortion $D$ (misidentification probability). Let $\pi$ be the attribute-profile map and let $A_\pi := \max_u |\{c : \pi(c)=u\}|$ be collision multiplicity. The main compression law is an exact ambiguity converse: any $D=0$ scheme must satisfy $L \ge \log_2 A_\pi$, and this lower bound is tight. The same law extends exactly to finite blocks: the minimum block tag budget is $L_t^\star = t\log_2 A_\pi$. The complementary query resource is governed by the distinguishing dimension $d$: without tags ($L=0$), zero-error identification requires $W=\Omega(d)$, and minimal sufficient query sets form the bases of a matroid, making $d$ an invariant of the observation structure rather than of a particular query strategy. In maximal-barrier domains ($A_\pi=k$), the nominal point $(L,W,D)=(\lceil\log_2 k\rceil,O(1),0)$ is the unique Pareto-optimal zero-error solution. We also study robustness in open worlds, where present collision-freedom is not extension-stable and not computably certifiable in full generality, and we derive implementation-level maintenance consequences and cross-domain instantiations across databases, biology, software runtimes, and model registries. The claims are accompanied by supplementary machine verification in Lean 4 (\LeanTotalLines\ lines, \LeanTotalTheorems\ theorem/lemma statements, \LeanTotalSorry\ \texttt{sorry}).

\textbf{Keywords:} semantic compression, zero-error compression, confusability graphs, rate-query tradeoff, matroid structure, zero-error identification
\end{abstract}
