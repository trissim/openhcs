\begin{abstract}
We study a deterministic zero-error side-information problem in which a decoder observes a fixed semantic representation $U=\pi(C)$ and an encoder transmits only the identity-resolving side information needed to recover the class $C$ exactly. The fixed-length zero-error law is tight and exact: if $A_\pi$ is the maximum representation-collision multiplicity, then any scheme that recovers $C$ with zero error requires at least $\log_2 A_\pi$ extra bits in the worst case, and this bound is tight. For repeated independent uses of the same representation map, the minimum block budget is exactly $L_t^\star=t\log_2 A_\pi$. We also study adaptive side information whose length may depend on the observed representation value: the pointwise optimal fiber budget is the ceiling logarithm of the corresponding fiber size, and any feasible adaptive scheme has expected length at least the expectation of that quantity, hence at least $H(C\mid U)/\log 2$. Under standard prefix-coding achievability within each fiber, there also exists an adaptive zero-error code with expected length at most $H(C\mid U)/\log 2 + 1$. Under a uniform source on a collision block of size $a$, subcritical fixed-length budgets satisfy the explicit error floor $D \ge 1-2^L/a$. 

Beyond the coding laws themselves, the same ambiguity-fiber geometry yields exact task-sufficiency, helper-view, and factorized-representation consequences for learned and neuro-symbolic systems. Crucially, we prove that semantic representation alone cannot achieve zero-error identity recovery when collisions exist---injective nominal handles are \emph{necessary} for lossless identity compression (\LH{NSL2}, \LH{NSL3}). The neurosymbolic linking theorem establishes that pairing a neural encoder (semantic representation) with a symbolic handle (identity-resolving side information) achieves zero-error identity if and only if the handle is injective (\LH{NSL1}, \LH{NSL4}, \LH{NSL5}). This architectural pattern---paying the identity bit budget through a nominal layer---is the canonical helper-view for open-world semantic systems (\LH{NSL6}).

\textbf{Keywords:} zero-error compression, side information, semantic identification, neurosymbolic linking, learned representations, confusability graphs
\end{abstract}
