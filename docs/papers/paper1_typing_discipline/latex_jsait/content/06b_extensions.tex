The main theorem chain identifies structural laws for zero-error identity recovery. This section records generalizations that relax specific model assumptions while preserving the core insight: residual ambiguity must be paid for in some resource currency.

\subsection{Stochastic and Semantic Generalizations}

The paper provides exact zero-error theory for fixed representations. Several extensions relax this deterministic assumption. With noisy representations or lossy side channels, deterministic fibers are replaced by probabilistic confusion neighborhoods, and the problem moves toward the graph-entropy regime \cite{korner1973coding}. In such settings, repeated sampling or increased side-information rate can reduce error, relaxing the exact fiber laws into probabilistic coding theorems.

A lossy extension would replace binary misidentification with semantic distortion measures such as hierarchical penalties or task-specific losses. This matters when classes carry internal geometry: the current theory distinguishes zero error from nonzero error, whereas a lossy theory must distinguish tolerable confusion from unacceptable failure. For learned representations, the theoretic task remains identifying what the resulting fiber statistics mean once they are estimated via discretization or empirical audits.

\subsection{Dynamic Growth and Trigger Conditions}

The static theorems also suggest operational limits for evolving systems. In a Poissonized growth model for representation cells with arrival rate $\lambda$, the probability of at least one collision in a cell is $1 - e^{-\lambda}(1+\lambda)$, which isolates the onset of identity pressure. The required exact identity budget becomes positive exactly when occupancy reaches two or more items per cell. \subsection{Empirical Audit: Embedding Collapse}

For learned representations, the fixed-length and adaptive fiber laws provide a formal measure of residual ambiguity.
